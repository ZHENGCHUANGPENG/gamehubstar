Run Of Life 3D Game Online

The menu opens on Level 1, with four gold nodes joined by a black bar under a pale sky. A gem counter reads zero at the top, and two cards sit on the sides: one promises a maximum lifetime of 60, the other faster upstairs speed, each priced at 20 gems. A yellow Start button waits in the middle of a blue sea. Pressing it pulls the camera back to a white track over the water, where a blonde girl stands at the start line and coloured gates wait ahead: Breakfast and Night Snack first, then Bibulosity and Light Soup, with Rice and Barbecue further up.

The listing gives the whole scheme in three words: slide to move. On a computer that means dragging with the mouse to steer the runner left and right along the track, and on a phone it means swiping. The gates are the decisions: run through a green gate or a red gate and the life ahead changes. Bottles sit on the track as pickups, and the two side cards trade gems for a longer life or quicker stairs.

The clearest strength is the idea itself: a whole life as a short run, with food and drink choices standing in for the decisions that shape it. The water, clouds and white track keep the screen calm, and the four-node bar makes progress easy to read. The downside is how little the first screen explains: the Start button stays on display after it is pressed, so the exact moment the run begins is not obvious, and the two gem cards sit at 20 gems while the counter starts at zero, which makes them feel far away on a first visit.

Casual runners and players who like life-sim ideas will enjoy the short, bright runs. Leave it alone if a runner must spell out every rule before the first step, or if portrait-only play is a problem.
