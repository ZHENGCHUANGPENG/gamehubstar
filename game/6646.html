Koenigsegg Jesko Absolut Slide Online

The game opens on a clean white screen with two plain choices: pick a difficulty, then pick a picture. Three grids wait under the first heading, 3x3, 4x4 and 5x5, and three photographs of the Koenigsegg Jesko Absolut wait under the second, one showing the silver car from the front on a road, one from the rear at speed, and one in a dark studio. Nothing else competes for attention, so the whole setup takes seconds. Tapping a difficulty tile lights it blue, and tapping a photo brings up a full-screen view of the car with a single large play button in the middle. It is a slide puzzle in the classic sense: the photo is cut into tiles, the tiles are shuffled, and the job is to slide them back into place.

The controls are the same on both kinds of device. A computer player clicks the difficulty tile, clicks a photo, then clicks the play button, and drags or clicks tiles to slide them around the grid. A phone player taps the same spots, and the game is built for a sideways screen, so the photo and the tiles sit side by side in a wide frame. Two small icons sit in the corners of the opening screen, one for sound and one for fullscreen, and both respond to a tap or a click. There is nothing hidden to learn before the first puzzle.

The strongest part is how little stands between the player and the puzzle: two choices and the game starts, and the car photos are sharp enough that the finished picture is worth reaching. The difficulty choice is honest too, since the 3x3 grid is a gentle start and the 5x5 grid is a real test of patience. The weak spot is the thinness of the package: three photos and three grid sizes are the whole game, and once a photo is solved there is no new twist waiting. The opening screen also leaves the rules unsaid, so a first-time player has to guess that the tiles slide rather than swap.

Car fans who like quiet puzzles will enjoy matching the Jesko piece by piece. Players who want timers, scores or a story should look elsewhere.

How to Play

On a computer, click a difficulty tile, then click one of the three car photos, then press the blue play button to start the puzzle. The tiles on the opening screen are 3x3, 4x4 and 5x5, and the 4x4 tile is highlighted when the screen first opens. On a phone, tap the same tiles and photos instead of clicking; the buttons are large and the puzzle fills a sideways screen. A speaker icon in the corner mutes the sound, and a fullscreen icon stretches the puzzle across the display.
