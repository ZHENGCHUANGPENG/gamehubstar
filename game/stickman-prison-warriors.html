Stickman Prison Warriors Online

One diagonal line decides whether the stickman walks free. Stickman Prison Warriors strips the escape genre down to a single verb: cut. Level 1 hangs a yellowed strip of paper over a gridded pit, parks your stickman on the left ledge, and lights an Exit door on the right. The white hand cursor — the game's only teacher — shows exactly where a swipe will slice, and the rest is geometry: sever the strip so it falls into steps or a ramp the little guy can climb. Search traffic may arrive expecting stickman hook unblocked, and it is worth saying plainly that no hooks, ropes, or swinging appear here. What does appear is a clean, readable physics sandbox: the scissors counter ticks at x3, the restart arrow begs to be abused, and the Pass button waits for the level that humiliates you. During testing, cut lines drew crisply under the finger, though the level refused to clear for an automated player — a reminder that the angles genuinely matter. The presentation is graph-paper minimalism, grey ledges on a blue grid, and it never wastes a pixel. For stickman hook unblocked fans willing to trade momentum for precision, this is a worthy detour — less adrenaline, more one more cut.

How to Play

Draw cut lines with a finger drag or a mouse click-and-drag across the hanging paper, exactly as the white tutorial hand cursor demonstrates. The scissors icon with its x3 counter tracks how many cuts the level allows, and the yellow circular arrow restarts the level when a cut goes wrong. A house icon returns to the menu, while the Pass button skips a level you cannot crack.

Tips

  • Study the gap between the stickman and the Exit before drawing — the cut has to drop the paper into a walkable shape, not just shred it.
  • The scissors counter shows x3, so treat every level as having limited cuts; sketch the line mentally before committing.
  • One bad angle ruins the drop, so hit the yellow restart arrow and try a shallower diagonal instead of forcing the same line.
  • The Pass button is not cheating — use it to skip a stubborn level and come back with fresh eyes.
