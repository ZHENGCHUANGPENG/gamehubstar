Angry Bird Friends Online

The game opens on a level map instead of a single title poster. Four tall cards stand in a row under a pale blue sky, above dark rocky ground edged with green grass. The first card, POACHED EGGS, shows green pigs in a nest, a crowned pig at the front, and a star counter reading 0 of 60. The second card, MIGHTY HOAX, and the third, DANGER ABOVE, both carry big silver padlocks, with 10 and 20 stars printed beneath them. The fourth card, GOLDEN EGGS, is a field of gold with a giant shining egg and no lock at all. A Unity WebGL credit sits at the bottom left and Angry Bird Friends is written at the bottom right. The listing frames the game around Star Cup tournaments, with new levels, one-on-one challenges, power-ups and leaderboards.

The control scheme is mouse only, because the instructions list nothing else. Click an episode card to open its levels, beginning with POACHED EGGS, the only card without a padlock. Stars collected in the open episode are the currency for the rest: MIGHTY HOAX asks for 10 and DANGER ABOVE asks for 20, both printed plainly under their padlocks. There is no keyboard key to learn and no second button on the map, so the first session is a matter of picking the open card and playing through its levels to bank stars.

The clearest strength is how readable the map is: four cards, one open, two locked with star prices printed on them, and a golden egg card that reads as a bonus. The familiar green pigs give the screen instant identity, and the star gating turns progress into a visible goal instead of a hidden menu. What holds it back is how little the map proves about the play itself, since no slingshot, no level and no bird appears on it, so the tournament claims in the listing stay unverified. The Unity load is also slow to reach the map, and the phone build shows only a blank blue screen, which is why the game is kept on computers.

Angry Birds fans who like episode maps, star collecting and unlocking new chapters with a mouse will enjoy the straightforward start. Leave it alone on phones, and leave it alone if the slingshot must be visible before picking an episode, or a leaderboard must sit on the first screen.
