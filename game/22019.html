Piano Master Online

Piano Master is a music game built on a familiar idea: pick a song and play it by touching tiles at the right moment. The song list is the first thing on screen, and it names six songs — HAPPY BIRTHDAY, LITTLE STAR, MERRY CHRISTMAS, PIECES, BEETHOVEN and CHILDHOOD — each row with a yellow music-note tile, a three-note difficulty mark and a green PLAY button. A big PIANO MASTER logo with a vinyl record sits above the list, so the whole game presents itself as a jukebox first and a challenge second.

The controls are tap or click, and the developer's own description is the guide here: choose the song you want to play, start the gameplay, and try to touch all the tiles at the right moment to play the song. Missing a tile ends the run, the developer says, so the timing is the whole game. Each song starts from its own green PLAY button, and the three-note mark on the row shows how hard that song is rated. The tile-tapping itself was not seen in testing, so this section reports the developer's description rather than an observed round.

The strongest part is the song list itself. Six named songs are visible from the start, the tunes are familiar enough to hum, and the difficulty mark on each row lets a player choose an easy song before trying a harder one. The vinyl-record logo and the yellow note tiles give the list a clear music identity. The honest limit is the evidence: only the song-select screen was seen, so the feel of the tiles, the speed of the songs and the timing window are all unverified. A player who wants to know how strict the timing is will have to try the first song and find out.

Good for music-game fans, and for players who like piano tiles with songs they already know. It fits brief sessions on a mobile or a desktop, and a quick tune beats a long practice piece here.

How to Play

On a computer, click the tiles as they come; a phone tap does the same. The developer describes the play this way: touch all the tiles at the right moment to play the song, and missing a tile ends the run. Each song row carries its own green PLAY button, so a song starts from its row, and the three-note mark on each row shows its difficulty.
