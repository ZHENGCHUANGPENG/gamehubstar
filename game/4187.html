Knock Balls Online

The title screen is a bold orange poster with one job: show the cannon and the balls. KNOCK BALLS runs across the top in big white bubble letters, with BEST SCORE: 0 underneath and LEVEL 1 in the middle, and TAP TO PLAY waits below it in the same white type. A white-and-gold cannon points up from the bottom centre, its barrel aimed at the sky, while a wooden crate of red-white striped balls sits at the lower left and wooden blocks frame the scene. The listing traces the game to the Can Knockdown series, promising physics-based carnival play with puzzle layouts and explosive action, and the title matches that pitch with the cannon and the balls front and centre.

The control scheme is a single sentence in the instructions: simply click on the screen. On a computer that means the mouse aims and fires the cannon, starting with TAP TO PLAY on the title screen. There is no keyboard key, no power meter and no second button on view, so the skill is all in where the click lands. That fits the carnival idea, since knocking balls down is about aim rather than combos. Phones are excluded, because the Unity player shows a not-supported warning on mobile, so the mouse is the only way to play.

The readable start is the best part: the cannon, the striped balls and the TAP TO PLAY line are all visible at once, and the orange background makes the white letters pop. The weak spot is how little the title reveals, since only the first level’s blocks are hinted at and no puzzle layout, target count or score target is shown. The best score sits at 0 with no leaderboard beside it, so there is nothing to chase on the title itself. The Unity mobile warning also cuts out phone players entirely, which is a real limit for a game that looks made for tapping.

Carnival physics fans and mouse-aim players who like one-click puzzle shooting on a computer will enjoy the quick start. It is a poor pick for phone sessions, for players who want the full puzzle list up front, or for anyone who needs a power meter before firing.
