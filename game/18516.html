The title screen is a bright cartoon race poster. FORMULA 1 DRIVER runs across the sky in red and white letters over a black paint splash, and red-white curbs edge the track above a strip of green grass. A big red play button with a white triangle sits on the right beside a smaller red steering-wheel button. The lower left carries a copyright line for FREEZE NOVA from 2021 with its website, and JulGames is written at the lower right. The listing pitches a formula racer where a car is customised and driven to first place against AI rivals across 30 levels.

The controls are a single line: arrow keys to control the car, which is all the instructions list, with the mouse used only to click the big red play button. That keeps the first minute simple, since the steering is the whole lesson and there is no gear, brake or boost key to memorise. The steering-wheel button beside play suggests a wheel option, but the keyboard arrows are the scheme the instructions name. Pick the play button, steer with the arrows, and learn the corners by feel.

The clean title is the best part: the green car, the palm trees and the grandstand read clearly in one glance, and the single red play button leaves no doubt where to click. What holds it back is how little the title shows of the racing itself, since there is no track choice, no car choice and no rival on view, so the customisation and the AI rivals the listing promises are still unproven from the screenshots. The copyright line also dates the game to 2021, so the art and the format feel older than the newest racers. The menu is a single poster with two buttons, which is charming but thin.

Formula racing fans who enjoy a simple keyboard setup and a quick start at a desk will like the clean title. Skip it for phone play, for players who want to see the car garage or the track list first, or for anyone who needs a tutorial before racing.
