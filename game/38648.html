Mahjong Tasty Pairs Online

Mahjong Tasty Pairs is a classic mahjong solitaire game with cartoon food tiles. The level map is the first thing you see after loading: fifteen levels in three rows of five, with only the first one open in orange and the rest locked behind grey padlocks. Each level shows three empty stars below it, and a counter at the top reads 0 out of 900 stars. A green play arrow sits at the bottom left, and a row of dots shows there are more pages of levels to come. The loading screen sets the look, with red and orange rays behind mahjong tiles showing Chinese characters, a ladybug tile and a tile with green dots.

The play is pairing. According to the listing, the goal is to clear each layout by finding identical open tiles and removing them in pairs, and that a tile counts as open when nothing covers it from above and it is not blocked on both sides at once. Blocked tiles are dimmed, so the moves you can actually make stand out from the ones you cannot. The listing also mentions shuffle, undo for the last pair and a hint for when the next move is hard to find.

The structure is the best part. The level map, the star counter and the locked levels give a plain matching game a clear sense of progress, and the food tiles make the board easy to read at a glance. The slow opening is the drawback: only one level is open at the start, the board itself only appears after the map, and the first screen is a loading image rather than tiles, so the game takes a moment to get going.

This one suits mahjong solitaire players who like a level map and a star to chase. Anyone after a single open table with no progression will find the map in the way.

How to Play

Select two identical open tiles to remove them from the board. Computer players click the first tile and then the matching one; a tile is available when no other tile covers it from above and it is not blocked on both side edges at the same time. On a phone, tap the same tiles; the level map is built for a phone held upright, with fifteen levels per page, three stars under each level, and a green play arrow at the bottom left. Blocked tiles are dimmed, so the available moves are easier to spot.
