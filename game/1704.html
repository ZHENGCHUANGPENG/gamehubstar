StickMan Team Force Online

StickMan Team Force opens on a night city street with three stickmen standing under a big STICKMAN TEAM FORCE sign. One carries a rifle, one a long gun and one a heavy weapon, and the street behind them has a green traffic light, shop fronts and a hotel sign glowing on the right. The Kiz10.com mark sits at the bottom, and three buttons wait below the heroes: a gamepad, a blue Play triangle and a film icon. The description frames the job plainly — a special force recruited to bring order to streets threatened by a high-technology organization — and the title art sells that job at a glance.

The controls are built around shooting and switching. On a computer, a mouse click or Z shoots, C changes hero, and Space fires the special move. On a phone the game wants landscape first: it shows a rotate-device prompt in portrait, then the same title and the same blue Play triangle appear once the phone is turned. Tapping Play opens a level select with six tiles, only the first one open, with arrows on the sides and a HOME button below. Tapping level 1 is the way into the fight.

The strongest part is how much the game shows before the first shot. Three distinct fighters, a readable night street and a level select that states its own progress make the setup feel finished. The honest limit is what testing could actually see: only the title and the level select were observed on both devices, so the street combat itself is described by the developer rather than confirmed in play. The portrait rotate prompt is also a small tax on phone players who open it upright.

Good for stickman shooter fans who like squad games and level-by-level progress, and for anyone after a short action session on a computer or a sideways phone. It suits quick runs where the goal is to clear the next level, not a long campaign.

How to Play

On a computer, click or press Z to shoot, press C to change hero, and press Space for the special move. On a phone, turn it sideways: the game asks for landscape, then tap the blue Play triangle and tap level 1. Shooting on a phone works by tapping the screen, the same way a mouse click shoots on a computer. The level select answers every tap, and only the first level is open at the start.
