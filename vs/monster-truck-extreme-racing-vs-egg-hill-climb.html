Monster Truck Extreme Racing vs Egg Hill Climb: Which is Better?

Two racing games, two kinds of driving. Monster Truck Extreme Racing is big-truck desert racing with nitro, a garage full of trucks to unlock, and a second player on the same keyboard. Egg Hill Climb is a small, pixel-art hill climb where two little cars escape together, collecting coins and reaching the finish as a team. Both are free racing games on GameHubStar and both were play-tested before they were listed — but one needs a computer, and the other goes anywhere.

01

How Monster Truck Extreme Racing plays

The menu opens on a garage scene with a bright green monster truck in the middle — big tyres, a raised body, a red detail on the side — the game’s orange logo in the top corner and a row of menu buttons down the left. The listing promises desert races where finishing in the top three wins prizes, a garage where new trucks can be bought, and a huge free-drive map for stunt shows, and the menu matches that picture: the truck is the star, and the garage is where the game wants you to spend time.

Races are about speed and nerve — save the nitro for the long desert straights, not the middle of a turn — and finishing in the top three earns prizes to spend on new trucks. Between races, the free-drive map is the place to learn the truck before a real race, and to show off stunts with nothing at stake. One- and two-player options are part of the listing, so a second driver can join on the same keyboard.

02

How Egg Hill Climb plays

Egg Hill Climb opens on a flat blue sky with blocky white clouds, EGG HILL CLIMB in chunky pixel letters across the top, and two wide buttons below — a green one with a white play triangle and a blue one with a shopping cart — with a tiny red car perched on the edge of each. A coin counter reads zero at the top right, and a speaker button and an info button sit at the corners.

The listing tells of two cars escaping together after a bank job, collecting coins on the way and reaching the finish as a team, and the two little cars on the menu match that idea. The driving is a balancing act: the pair moves as a team, so keeping both cars close together matters more than speed, and the coins along the way are worth picking up — the shop button opens a blue panel beside the play button. Tapping the green play button brings up a skippable pre-game ad, the usual interstitial before the run begins.

03

Controls and devices

This is where the two games split. Monster Truck Extreme Racing is keyboard-only and listed for computers: Player 1 drives with the arrow keys, presses N for nitro, B to look back and R to restart a level, while Player 2 uses W, A, S and D to drive, T for nitro and C to look back. It cannot be played on a phone or tablet.

Egg Hill Climb works everywhere: on a desktop you drive with WASD for one car and the arrow keys for the other, balancing each one over the hills, and on a phone the on-screen mobile controls adjust each car and its balance. Both are landscape games — rotate a phone sideways for the full view.

04

Head to head

Speed vs balance. Monster Truck is about going fast and using nitro at the right moment. Egg Hill Climb is about keeping two cars upright over hills — slow down and you stall, rush and you flip.

Progress. Monster Truck unlocks new trucks in the garage with race prizes. Egg Hill Climb spends collected coins in its shop.

Playing together. Monster Truck has a real two-player mode on one keyboard — two drivers, two sets of keys. Egg Hill Climb is a team of two cars driven by one player.

Where you can play. Monster Truck needs a computer with a keyboard. Egg Hill Climb works on desktop, phone and tablet.

Ratings. Both games carry a 7.5 rating in our listing, and both earned it in play-testing — Monster Truck’s garage menu, green truck and keyboard controls were checked on a computer, and Egg Hill Climb’s pixel-art menu, two cars and play button were checked on desktop and on a phone.

05

The verdict

Pick Monster Truck Extreme Racing if you play on a computer and want speed, stunts and competition — nitro on the straights, prizes in the garage, and a friend on the other half of the keyboard. Pick Egg Hill Climb if you play on a phone, or if you want a relaxed drive: two little cars, a pocketful of coins, and a hill that only asks you not to flip. If you are on a phone, the choice is already made — Egg Hill Climb is the only one of the two that runs there. On a computer, take the truck for thrills and the egg for a quiet evening.
